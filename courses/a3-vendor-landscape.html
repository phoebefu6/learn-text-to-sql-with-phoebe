The buy-side session

You will not build a text-to-SQL system - you will buy one, or approve someone who does. That makes your job vendor triage: seeing past demo polish and non-comparable benchmarks to the two questions that actually predict success - where does the data go, and who pays the curation tax (session a4). This session gives you the landscape and the bake-off discipline to choose well.

The two warehouse-native flagships 9 min live

If your data already lives in Databricks or Snowflake, the default choice is that platform's native tool - the data never leaves, and governance comes along for free. Here is how the two leaders actually differ under the marketing.

 Databricks AI/BI GenieSnowflake Cortex Analyst

Pricing shapeNo per-question AI charge; you pay for the SQL warehousePer message processed, plus warehouse cost

Read those last two numbers as marketing, not measurement Both claims come from each vendor's own internal benchmark, on their own data, with their own scoring. They are not comparable to each other and not predictive of your results. Treat every "90%+" as "90%+ on questions they chose."
LiveThe one thing both have in common: heavy curation4 min▶

Neither tool works out of the box, and both vendors quietly admit it. A fresh Genie space was reported to score 8/15 (53%) before systematic remodeling brought it to 15/15. Cortex Analyst's accuracy lives or dies on the quality of the YAML semantic model a human writes. The tool is the cheap part - the metadata and metric definitions are the real project, which is exactly session a4's curation tax.

  • Genie's own recipe: 3-7 tables per room, 3-5 trusted example queries, roughly two weeks of iteration before it is trustworthy.
  • Cortex's lever: the semantic model. A rich, correct YAML is where the 90% claim comes from; a thin one lands far lower.
  • Both degrade on open-ended questions that diverge from the curated examples - the demo questions always look great, the eleventh novel question often does not.
Real world

"We already pay for Snowflake, so Cortex is free." A common and expensive misread. The licence may be bundled, but the person-months your data team spends writing and maintaining the semantic model are the actual cost - and they recur every time the schema changes. The tool decision is easy; the curation commitment is the real budget line, and it belongs in the business case up front.

The wider field, by category 7 min live

Beyond the two warehouse flagships, the market splits into three more categories. Knowing which box a vendor is in tells you more than any feature list, because the box determines the governance and accuracy trade-offs.

Self-studyThe gotchas hiding in each category2 min read▶
  • BI copilots inherit your existing semantic model if you have one (a Looker or Power BI dataset) - which is a genuine head start, but also means their accuracy is capped by how good that model already is.
  • AI analysts like Hex Magic index your warehouse schema and notebook context but, per practitioner reports, still hallucinate join keys on messy schemas. Julius-style CSV tools run Python in a sandbox with no governance and no row-level security - convenient and a shadow-IT risk. Querio is a data point on price: reported at roughly $14k/year.
  • Open-source gives control and no per-seat fee, at the cost of you hosting and maintaining it - and market volatility is real: Vanna, the most-starred OSS framework (~23.8k stars), archived its repo in March 2026 and pivoted to a commercial cloud. WrenAI remains active with a governance-focused platform.
Guided exercise

Draft your five-line bake-off ★ 10 min · with your team

The single most valuable thing you can do before signing anything: refuse the vendor's benchmark and run your own head-to-head on your own data. Here is the whole protocol - five lines, and it beats any analyst report.

Pick 20 real questions your business actually asks - pulled from the BI backlog, including the ambiguous-metric ones ("revenue" - gross or delivered?).

Have a human write the correct SQL for each. This is your golden set, and it is reusable forever.

Run all 20 on each finalist against the same warehouse, with the same curation effort budget for each.

Count silent failures by hand - plausible answers that are wrong. That number, not the vendor's, is your real accuracy.

Then compare curation hours and price. The cheapest tool that clears your accuracy bar with the least curation wins.

Why this beats the demo A vendor demo is a curated success on questions they chose. Your bake-off is an honest measurement on questions you chose. The gap between the two is exactly the risk you would otherwise carry into production - and session a5 shows why that gap is a governance problem, not just a quality one.
Homework

Questions to bring to your team ◐ 20-30 min total

  • Which category are we actually shopping in - warehouse-native, BI copilot, AI analyst, or open-source? What determines it (where our data lives)?
  • For every vendor accuracy claim we have been shown, ask: on whose data, whose questions, whose scoring? Write down how many are comparable. (Answer: none.)
  • Do we have 20 real questions with human-verified SQL we could run as a bake-off? If not, that is the first deliverable, before any trial.
  • If we picked our warehouse's native tool, who on the data team owns the semantic model or curator instructions, and for how many hours a month?
  • What is our exposure if a chosen open-source tool gets archived like Vanna did - could we self-maintain, or would we be stranded?

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Two vendors both claim "90%+ accuracy." How should a leader read that?

Genie's 32%-to-90% and Cortex's 90%-vs-51% both come from separate internal benchmarks. They cannot be compared to each other or to your environment. Run your own golden set.

2 · What is the core difference in how Genie and Cortex Analyst ground their answers?

Both are curation-first, but the shape differs: Genie curator instructions and example queries versus Cortex's YAML semantic model ("the magic is in the YAML"). Either way, a human writes the context.

3 · What is the one thing to demand in any vendor evaluation?

Your own golden set is the only benchmark that predicts your results. Counting silent failures by hand surfaces the plausible-but-wrong answers a demo hides - the real risk.
